# Complaint resolution case studies

These are fictional practice scenarios. They show a reasoned approach, not real employment outcomes.

## Case 1: Duplicate charge concern

**Situation:** A customer reports seeing two charges for one purchase.

**Acknowledge:** “I can see why two entries would be concerning. I’ll help check what each one represents.”

**Investigate:** Verify the order and payment records using approved tools. Determine whether one entry is a pending authorization or a settled duplicate. Never ask for a full card number or security code.

**Resolve:** Explain the verified status in plain language. If a duplicate settled charge is confirmed, follow the organization's refund procedure and give the customer a reference and realistic update window drawn from policy.

**Close the loop:** Summarize what was checked, what action was taken, and when/how the customer will hear back. Escalate a confirmed billing error through the correct channel.

## Case 2: Damaged item received

**Situation:** A customer receives a damaged item and is upset about having to resolve it.

**Acknowledge:** Apologize for the experience without blaming the customer or making an unverified promise.

**Investigate:** Confirm the order and ask for the minimum evidence required by the returns policy, using a private support channel.

**Resolve:** Offer the available policy-based options, such as replacement or refund, and clearly explain any required steps.

**Close the loop:** Confirm the selected option, expected next update, and who owns the follow-up. Escalate if the normal path is unavailable or delayed.

## Principles used

- Listen and reflect the customer's concern.
- Gather only information needed to investigate.
- Separate verified facts from possibilities.
- Set an expectation and keep it.
- Escalate issues that exceed authority or policy.
