# Customer response samples

All scenarios and names below are fictional practice examples.

## 1. Order has not arrived

**Customer:** “My order was meant to arrive yesterday. Can you tell me where it is?”

**Response:**

Hi Maya, I’m sorry your order hasn’t arrived when expected. I can check the latest tracking update for you. Please send your order number through our secure support channel; for your privacy, don’t post payment details here. Once I’ve checked the status, I’ll explain the next step and when you can expect another update.

**Why this works:** Acknowledges the inconvenience, asks only for relevant information, protects sensitive details, and sets a clear follow-up expectation without promising an unverified delivery date.

## 2. Difficulty signing in

**Customer:** “I’ve tried to reset my password twice and the link keeps failing.”

**Response:**

Hi Daniel, that sounds frustrating. Let’s get you back into your account. Please request one new reset link and open the most recent email in the same browser where you use the service. If it still fails, tell me the exact error message and roughly when you requested it. Please don’t send your password or a reset link. I’ll use that information to check the next troubleshooting step.

**Why this works:** Offers a simple first step, requests useful diagnostic detail, and avoids collecting credentials.

## 3. Refund timing question

**Customer:** “You said my refund was processed, but I still don’t see it.”

**Response:**

Hi Amina, I understand why you’re checking. I can verify the refund status and the date it was issued. The time for it to appear can depend on the original payment provider, so I don’t want to guess at a date. Please share your order reference through the secure channel, and I’ll confirm what we can see and what to do if it remains missing.

**Why this works:** Avoids unsupported timelines and explains how the issue will be checked.
